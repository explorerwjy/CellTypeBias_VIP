# Specificity Cap — Reviewer Reply & Methods Text

## Reviewer Comment

> Clarify why the cell-type specificity score is capped at 2. How sensitive are results to this capping, and why not use previously established specificity metrics?

## Reviewer Reply

We thank the reviewer for raising this point. The specificity cap at 2x mean fold-enrichment serves as a conservative guard against technical inflation in low-sequencing-depth cell types. In our atlas of 461 cell types, uncapped specificity values reach up to ~97x, driven almost entirely by small non-neuronal populations (e.g., vascular, fibroblast, microglia) with low total UMI. Without capping, 56.7% of the total specificity sum in non-neuronal cell types comes from genes exceeding the threshold, compared to only 15.4% for neuronal types (Spearman rho between UMI depth and fraction of genes exceeding cap = -0.497, p = 3.6e-30). These extreme values reflect sparse sampling artifacts rather than genuine expression enrichment. To assess sensitivity, we recomputed SCZ mutation bias under four cap levels (1x, 2x, 3x, and uncapped). Cap = 3x produces nearly identical rankings to the default 2x, confirming robustness, while cap = 1x over-clips and reduces discriminability among cell types. Without any cap, poorly-sampled cell types acquire spurious extreme bias scores. We present these analyses in new Supplementary Figure Sx. Regarding previously established metrics (e.g., tau, pSI), our fold-enrichment approach has the advantage of being directly interpretable in terms of expression magnitude and compatible with weighted-sum bias calculations, whereas rank-based metrics discard quantitative information needed for the weighted analysis framework.

## Methods / Supplementary Notes

Expression specificity is defined as fold-enrichment: S(g, ct) = TPM(g, ct) / mean(TPM(g)), where values > 1 indicate above-average expression in a given cell type. To prevent technical artifacts from dominating bias estimates, specificity values are capped at 2x the global mean specificity (~2.0). This threshold affects 5.6% of all gene-by-cell-type values and disproportionately impacts non-neuronal cell types with low sequencing depth: on average 13.7% of genes exceed the cap in non-neuronal types versus 3.9% in neuronal types (Mann-Whitney U, p = 2.7e-45). The inflation arises because cell types with low total UMI produce noisier TPM estimates, and the fold-enrichment calculation amplifies this noise into extreme specificity scores (up to ~97x in uncapped data). A sensitivity analysis varying the cap from 1x to 3x and uncapped demonstrates that mutation bias rankings are robust: cap = 3x yields nearly identical results to the default 2x, while removing the cap allows small, poorly-sampled cell types to acquire spuriously high rankings driven by a few genes with inflated specificity (Supplementary Figure Sx).
